# Candidates — The Dog Reviews the Mailman

Brief: one funny one-line review a dog gives the mailman, under 200
characters. The house judge compares two lines at a time on: the dog's
voice, the mailman's visit, and the punchline (grudge, bribe, suspicion
or small triumph, with a turn at the end that earns the laugh).

## The 10 candidates

### 1. ⭐ FINAL PICK
> The mailman is my nemesis, my cardio, and my alarm clock. I bark, he runs, I eat the coupons. I hate him. I'd miss him terribly. Five stars.
> (140 characters)

**Why it wins:** every judge criterion in one line. Dog's voice: "nemesis,
my cardio, and my alarm clock" — the visit is his whole daily routine.
Small triumph: "I bark, he runs" plus the dog priority of eating the
coupons. The turn: "I hate him. I'd miss him terribly." — grudging love
nobody else can copy — then the punchline "Five stars" lands the
contradiction. Tight, complete, specific to this dog and this mailman.

### 2.
> One star. He shows up every day, shoves paper through MY slot, and leaves before I can finish my bark. Rude. I will be here tomorrow.
> (133 characters)

**Why it's good:** strong grudge voice, "MY slot" is territorial and very
dog, "leaves before I can finish my bark" is a fresh complaint. Weaker
than #1: the ending ("I will be here tomorrow") is a shrug, not a turn.

### 3.
> Five stars. I bark, he runs, the mail lands on the floor, and I get to eat the coupons. Best part of my day, every day.
> (119 characters)

**Why it's good:** pure dog priorities — eating the coupons is the funniest
dog detail here, and the triumph is clear. Weaker than #1: no turn at the
end, it just states the routine; several rivals will write a version of
"bark, he runs, five stars".

### 4.
> He knocks once, I lose my mind, he leaves the good stuff and runs. Honestly? The highlight of my day. Would bark again.
> (119 characters)

**Why it's good:** "Would bark again" is a neat review-format closer and
"the highlight of my day" is honest dog logic. Weaker than #1: "I lose my
mind" is generic; the middle sags.

### 5.
> Suspicious man in the same blue shirt visits daily, stuffs envelopes in my door, never once brings a biscuit. Two stars, and I'm being generous.
> (144 characters)

**Why it's good:** suspicion angle, "never once brings a biscuit" is the
bribe angle done right, "I'm being generous" is a good reviewer voice.
Weaker than #1: it reads like a person complaining, less dog in the
vocabulary; the punchline is mild.

### 6.
> Dear mailman: the porch is mine, the slot is mine, and the barking is non-negotiable. The tail wagging, however, is strictly confidential.
> (138 characters)

**Why it's good:** "the tail wagging is strictly confidential" is a lovely
turn — secret affection the dog won't admit. Weaker than #1: the opening
is a list of claims rather than a scene, and "Dear mailman" softens the
dog voice.

### 7.
> Every day at noon he dares to approach MY house with paper. Every day I chase him off. He keeps coming back. I respect the hustle. One star.
> (140 characters)

**Why it's good:** grudge plus reluctant respect ("I respect the hustle")
is a funny arc, and the daily ritual is clear. Weaker than #1: "One star"
after respect undercuts the joke instead of landing it.

### 8.
> He brings the mail, I bring the barking, and somehow I'M the one in trouble? Five stars for the drama, zero for the treats.
> (123 characters)

**Why it's good:** "somehow I'M the one in trouble?" is a great dog
grievance, and the split rating is a neat review joke. Weaker than #1:
"the drama" is a human word, and the line is more complaint than scene.

### 9.
> I have barked at this man 847 times. He has never once stayed to play. Commitment issues? Or just afraid of greatness. Either way: one star.
> (140 characters)

**Why it's good:** the exact count (847) is funny and specific, "afraid of
greatness" is a confident dog ego. Weaker than #1: it leans on the number
for the laugh and the ending is a flat one-star shrug.

### 10.
> Review of the mailman: arrives daily, smells like other dogs' houses, leaves paper I can't read. Still, he is the only visitor I get. Three stars.
> (146 characters)

**Why it's good:** "smells like other dogs' houses" is a wonderfully
dog-specific suspicion, and "the only visitor I get" is sad-funny.
Weaker than #1: three separate observations, no single turn; the ending
is wistful rather than a punchline.

## Final choice

**Candidate 1** — it is the only one that scores on all five of the
judge's criteria at once: on-topic review, the dog's side of the visit
(grudge + triumph), unmistakable dog voice and priorities, a tight line
with a real turn at the end, and a laugh that belongs to this dog and
this mailman (the coupons, the alarm clock, the missed nemesis).
